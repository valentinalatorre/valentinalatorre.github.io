About

I'm an astrophysicist interested in understanding how galaxies form and evolve across cosmic time, and in using machine learning and data analysis to study the Universe through large astronomical surveys.

I earned my PhD in Physics (Astrophysics) from Tufts University in 2026, where I worked with Prof. Anna Sajina and Prof. Danilo Marchesini as a Kathryn A. McCarthy Graduate Fellow. During my PhD, I was also a Visiting Student Fellow at the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian.

Outside research, I enjoy exploring new places, playing the piano, spending time in nature, making fresh pasta, and doing improv.

Research

Galaxy evolution

My research investigates galaxy formation, stellar mass assembly, and star-formation quenching using multiwavelength observations from Subaru-HSC-Deep, CLAUDS, UltraVISTA, and Spitzer/IRAC, alongside cosmological simulations and semi-analytic models.

Machine Learning & Statistical Modeling

I develop and apply machine learning techniques, including Self-Organizing Maps (SOMs), Random Forests, XGBoost, and neural networks, to estimate galaxy properties from photometric data. My work also involves spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting, Bayesian inference, Monte Carlo methods, and statistical modeling, with particular attention to observational uncertainties and missing data.

Black holes

I developed HOLESOM, a machine-learning framework to estimate the masses and accretion rates of massive black holes, including slowly accreting systems, using limited multiwavelength observations.
